Rebecca Close makes animation films, installations, writes essays, academic articles, poetry and experimental texts, weaving a digital arts practice with personal/collective narratives to reclaim ‘social reproduction’ as a site of imagination and possibility.
Their work explores the role of digital/networked/computational cultures in shaping changing perceptions of family, households, sex-gender and sexuality norms, healthcare and borders.
Their research focuses on how identity categories and population concerns are enacted in the scientific study of reproductive biology and addresses the increasing participation of financial actors and market forces in making reproductive knowledge.
They are currently based between Institut d'Historia de la Ciencia (UAB, Barcelona) and School of Applied Social Studies (UCC, Cork) working on a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions project on the use of animation across bioscience and contemporary art.
